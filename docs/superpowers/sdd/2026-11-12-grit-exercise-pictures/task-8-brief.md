- Laziness rule (Figures.kt comment): an area's map must not build its figures (map of `Lazy<ExerciseFigure>`); AreaMapsLazyTest must keep passing.
- Pins/spotter only where the guide's safety line asks (constraints.md).
- Camera: lateral raises, reverse flys and rear-delt raises are about arms moving out to the sides — FRONT view is the natural camera for the standing/seated ones (as for the chest's cable crossover and the back's shrugs); a bent-over rear-delt raise seen from the side hides the arms, so judge front vs side by which shows the raise (for bent-over ones a front view shows the head-on torso with arms out: see the lesson on head-on bodies). Front raises and presses read from the side; the Arnold press's rotation (palms toward you at the bottom, away at the top) should be visible in the dumbbell orientation (end-on vs profile). Rotations: the upper arm stays at the side with the elbow at 90° and the forearm swings in or out — choose the camera that shows the swing (a view from the front shows the forearm foreshortened pointing at the camera, then out to the side; say why you chose it).
- Key checks per entry: presses → the guide's start (bar/dumbbells/handles at the guide's height: chin, shoulders, upper chest) and lockout over the shoulders, seat/back-pad contacts, feet on the floor; raises → the top height the guide gives (level with shoulders — not above unless the guide says), the fixed elbow bend, straight torso (or the guide's lean/hinge angle), start at the sides/in front of the thighs; reverse flys/rear-delt raises → hinge or pad contact, arms out to shoulder level, fixed bend; rear-delt row → elbows high and out; rotations → elbow at the side (within a few cm) and bent 90° in both panels, the forearm's swing between the guide's end points. Each entry gets a focused test in a new `BatchShouldersTest.kt` with real bounds. The arm checks for out-of-plane arms are looser since Task 6 (see the Body.kt comment): for any arm with `out != 0`, add an explicit bound in the figure's own test that would catch a wrong-way elbow.
