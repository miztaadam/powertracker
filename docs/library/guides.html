Barbell floor press

Also called: Floor press Equipment: Barbell Muscles: Pecs, Triceps

A barbell press lying on the floor inside a power rack. Your upper arms touch the floor at the bottom, which is said to take away the stretch and rebound of a normal bench.

Setup

  • Lie on the floor inside a power rack, under the bar, with your knees bent.
  • Set the safety pins, or use a spotter.
  • Grip the bar overhand, a little wider than your shoulders.
  • Breathe in and brace before you lift the bar out.

How to do it

  1. Lift the bar out of the rack and hold it over your chest with straight arms.
  2. Lower the bar until your upper arms touch the floor.
  3. Pause there for a moment.
  4. Press the bar up until your arms are straight.
  5. Put the bar back in the rack after the last rep.

Cues

  • Upper arms touch, then pause.
  • Press from a still start.

Stretch and squeeze (bodybuilding side)

  • Stretch: Your chest is at its longest as your upper arms touch the floor.
  • Squeeze: Squeeze your chest as you press to straight arms.

Common mistakes

  • Bouncing your arms off the floor.
  • Pressing before your upper arms touch the floor.

Stop if you feel sharp pain. For an injury or a condition, check with a doctor or physiotherapist.

Bench

Also called: Bench press, Barbell bench press Equipment: Barbell Muscles: Pecs, Triceps, Front delts

The competition bench press. It builds pressing strength in your chest, shoulders and triceps.

Setup

  • Set the safety pins, or use a spotter.
  • Lie on the bench with your head, shoulders and buttocks on the bench. Plant your feet flat on the floor.
  • Grip the bar a little wider than your shoulders.
  • Keep a slight arch in your back.
  • Breathe in and brace before you lift the bar out.

How to do it

  1. Lift the bar out and hold it over your shoulders with straight arms.
  2. Lower the bar under control to your chest.
  3. Pause with the bar still on your chest.
  4. Press the bar up until your elbows lock.
  5. Put the bar back in the rack after the last rep.

Cues

  • Feet stay planted.
  • Head, shoulders and buttocks stay on the bench.
  • Pause on your chest before you press.

Stretch and squeeze (bodybuilding side)

  • Stretch: Your chest stretches in the pause with the bar on your chest.
  • Squeeze: Squeeze your chest as you press the bar up.

Common mistakes

  • Bouncing the bar off your chest.
  • Lifting your buttocks off the bench.
  • Gripping so wide that the range of motion gets short.

Stop if you feel sharp pain. For an injury or a condition, check with a doctor or physiotherapist.

Block pull

Also called: Deadlift off blocks Equipment: Barbell Muscles: Glutes, Upper back

A deadlift from a bar resting on blocks. It builds strength in the middle of the pull and lets you use more weight.

Setup

  • Set the plates on sturdy blocks, about 5, 10 or 15 cm high.
  • Take your normal deadlift stance under the bar.
  • Grip the bar shoulder-width or a little wider, palms down or one up and one down. Chalk or straps can help your grip.
  • Keep your hips low, your shoulders high, and your arms and back straight.
  • Brace your trunk before you pull.

How to do it

  1. Stand up by straightening your hips and knees. Keep the bar close to your legs.
  2. Stand tall with your knees locked.
  3. Lower the bar back to the blocks under control.

Cues

  • Bar close.
  • Back straight.
  • Knees point the same way as your feet.

Stretch and squeeze (bodybuilding side)

  • Squeeze: Squeeze your glutes as you stand tall with your knees locked.

Common mistakes

Box squat

Also called: Barbell box squat Equipment: Barbell Muscles: Glutes, Hips

A squat to a box set at your target depth. It teaches you to sit back and stand up from a still start.

Setup

  • Set the bar in the rack just below shoulder height.
  • Set the safety pins, or use a spotter.
  • Place a sturdy box behind you at the height of your target depth.
  • Step under the bar and rest it across your upper back.
  • Lift the bar out and step back until the box is just behind you.
  • Set your feet in your normal squat stance.
  • Take a big breath into your belly and brace your trunk before each rep.

How to do it

  1. Sit back by bending your hips and knees. Keep your shins close to upright.
  2. Sit on the box under control. Don't drop onto it.
  3. Pause briefly on the box while staying braced.
  4. Drive up from the box to standing.

Cues

  • Sit back, not down.
  • Stay braced on the box.
  • Shins close to upright.

Stretch and squeeze (bodybuilding side)

  • Stretch: Your glutes stretch as you sit back onto the box.
  • Squeeze: Squeeze your glutes as you drive up to standing.

Common mistakes

  • Dropping onto the box.
  • Relaxing on the box and losing your brace.

Deadlift

Also called: Conventional deadlift, Barbell deadlift Equipment: Barbell Muscles: Hamstrings, Glutes, Lats

The competition deadlift. It builds strength in your hips, legs and back.

Setup

  • Stand with your feet flat under the bar, about shoulder-width apart, with your shins close to it.
  • Bend down and grip the bar shoulder-width or a little wider. Use both palms down, or one up and one down.
  • Pull your shoulders down and back, and lift your chest.
  • Brace your trunk before you pull.

How to do it

  1. Push through your heels to start the lift.
  2. Keep the bar close to your legs as it rises. Raise it at the same rate as your hips.
  3. Stand tall with your knees locked and your shoulders back.
  4. Lower the bar under control by pushing your hips back and bending your knees.
  5. Let the bar settle on the floor before the next rep.

Cues

  • Keep the bar close.
  • Lift with your hips, not just your back.
  • Keep your back flat.

Stretch and squeeze (bodybuilding side)

  • Stretch: Your hamstrings stretch as you push your hips back to lower.
  • Squeeze: Squeeze your glutes as you stand tall with your knees locked.

Common mistakes

  • Rounding your back.
  • Lifting with your back instead of your hips.
  • Letting the bar drift away from your legs.

Stop if you feel sharp pain. For an injury or a condition, check with a doctor or physiotherapist.

Front squat

Also called: Barbell front squat Equipment: Barbell Muscles: Quads, Glutes, Upper back, Trunk

A squat with the bar on the front of your shoulders. It puts more of the work on your quads and needs an upright trunk.

Setup

  • Set the bar in the rack at upper-chest height.
  • Set the safety pins, or use a spotter.
  • Grip the bar about shoulder-width apart and step under it.
  • Rest the bar across the front of your shoulders, palms facing up and elbows pointing straight ahead.
  • Set your feet in your normal squat stance.
  • Take a big breath into your belly and brace your trunk before each rep.

How to do it

  1. Lift the bar out and step back.
  2. Squat down, keeping your torso upright and your back straight.
  3. Go down until your thighs are level with the floor. Stop sooner if your heels lift or your back starts to round.
  4. Drive up by straightening your hips and knees.
  5. Put the bar back in the rack after the last rep.

Cues

  • Elbows point straight ahead.
  • Weight over your whole foot.
  • Knees in line with your toes.

Stretch and squeeze (bodybuilding side)

  • Stretch: Your quads stretch at the bottom of your squat.
  • Squeeze: Squeeze your quads as your hips and knees straighten.

Common mistakes

  • Keeping your shins too upright, so your torso tips forward.
  • Your back starting to round.
  • Heels lifting off the floor.

A seated heel raise with a weight across your thighs and your feet on a step. It works your calves with your knees bent, so it needs no machine.

Setup

  • Sit on a bench with both feet on a step and your heels hanging off the edge.
  • Put a dumbbell or a plate across the tops of your thighs.
  • Make sure the balls of your feet are firmly on the step.
  • Brace your trunk before each rep.

How to do it

  1. Slowly lower your heels below the edge of the step.
  2. Raise your heels by pointing your ankles.

Cues

  • Slow on the way down.
  • Balls of your feet stay on the step.

Common mistakes

  • Bouncing out of the bottom, so momentum does the work.
  • Letting your feet slip on the step. Stop and reset them if they move.

Stop if you feel sharp pain. For an injury or a condition, check with a doctor or physiotherapist.

Single-leg calf raise

Also called: One-leg calf raise, Single-leg heel raise, Dumbbell single-leg calf raise Equipment: Bodyweight, Dumbbell Muscles: Calves

A heel raise on one foot on a step. It works each calf on its own.

Setup

  • Stand on a step on one foot, with your heel hanging off the edge. Make sure the ball of your foot is firmly on the step.
  • Hold a support for balance. To make it harder, hold a dumbbell in the hand on the same side as your working leg.
  • Brace your trunk before each rep.

How to do it

  1. Start standing on the step on one foot, heel hanging off the edge.
  2. Slowly lower your heel below the edge of the step.
  3. Raise your heel by pointing your ankle, keeping your knee straight.
  4. Do all your reps on one leg, then switch.

Cues

  • Knee straight.
  • Use the support for balance only.

Common mistakes

  • Bending your knees to push the weight up, so your thighs do the work.
  • Bouncing out of the bottom, so momentum does the work.
  • Letting your foot slip on the step. Stop and reset it if it moves.

Stop if you feel sharp pain. For an injury or a condition, check with a doctor or physiotherapist.

Smith calf raise

Also called: Smith machine calf raise, Smith standing calf raise Equipment: Smith machine Muscles: Calves

A standing heel raise with the Smith machine bar across your upper back. It works your calves with your knees straight.

Setup

  • Set the bar at upper-chest height, with a calf block under it.
  • Set the safety stops just below your lowest position.
  • Step under the bar and rest it across your upper back. Hold the bar at the sides.
  • Put the balls of your feet on the calf block, with your heels hanging off the edge.
  • Brace your trunk before each rep.
  • Stand up and unhook the bar by turning it back off the hooks.

How to do it

  1. Raise your heels by pointing your ankles.
  2. Slowly lower your heels below the edge of the block.
  3. After the last rep, turn the bar forward onto the hooks.

Cues

  • Knees straight, or only slightly bent.
  • Slow on the way down.

Common mistakes

  • Bending your knees to push the weight up, so your thighs do the work.
  • Bouncing out of the bottom, so momentum does the work.
  • Letting your feet slip on the block. Stop and reset them if they move.

Stop if you feel sharp pain. For an injury or a condition, check with a doctor or physiotherapist.

Ab wheel rollout

Also called: Ab wheel, Wheel rollout, Ab roller, Ab roller rollout, Kneeling wheel rollout Equipment: Bodyweight Muscles: Abs

Rolling an ab wheel forward from your knees and back again. It trains your abs to keep your trunk from sagging.

Setup

  • Kneel on the floor and hold the wheel's handles.
  • Put the wheel on the floor near the front of your knees, arms straight.
  • Brace your trunk before each rep.

How to do it

  1. Roll the wheel forward, lowering your body toward the floor.
  2. Stop before your hips start to sag or your lower back starts to arch.
  3. Pull the wheel back to your knees by bending at your hips.

Cues

  • Elbows straight or nearly straight.
  • Trunk stiff.

Common mistakes

  • Letting your lower back sag or arch.
  • Bending your elbows.

Stop if you feel sharp pain. For an injury or a condition, check with a doctor or physiotherapist.

Bicycle crunch

Also called: Bicycle crunches, Supine bicycle crunch Equipment: Bodyweight Muscles: Obliques, Abs

A crunch with a twist, bringing each elbow toward the opposite knee in turn. It works your abs and obliques.

Setup

  • Lie on your back with your knees bent, feet flat and your hands behind your head.
  • Brace your trunk before each rep.
  • Lift both feet until your thighs are upright, with your knees bent to about 90 degrees.

How to do it

  1. Curl your upper body up and turn it so one elbow moves toward the opposite knee, while you straighten your other leg.
  2. Switch sides, bringing the other elbow toward the other knee.

Cues

  • Lower back pressed to the floor.
  • Turn from your trunk, not your hips.
  • Slow and controlled.

Common mistakes

  • Pulling your head forward with your hands.
  • Letting your lower back lift off the floor.
  • Rushing the reps.

Stop if you feel sharp pain. For an injury or a condition, check with a doctor or physiotherapist.

Bird dog

Also called: Birddog, Quadruped arm and leg raise Equipment: Bodyweight Muscles: Abs, Lower back

Raising one arm and the opposite leg from your hands and knees. It trains your trunk to stay still while your limbs move.

Setup

  • Get on your hands and knees, knees and feet hip-width apart.
  • Brace your trunk to keep your back in its natural line, not sagging or arched.

How to do it

  1. Raise one leg and the opposite arm toward level with the floor. Raise them only as high as you can keep your lower back still.
  2. Gently lower them back to the start, then switch sides.

Cues

  • Hips and shoulders level.
  • Lower back still.

Common mistakes

  • Letting your hips tilt as your leg lifts.
  • Raising your arm or leg so high that your lower back arches.

Stop if you feel sharp pain. For an injury or a condition, check with a doctor or physiotherapist.

Cable woodchop

Also called: Woodchop, Wood chop, Cable chop, Cable wood chop, Standing rotational chop, High-to-low chop Equipment: Cable Muscles: Obliques, Abs

Sweeping a cable handle from above one shoulder down to the opposite hip. It trains your trunk and hips to turn together.

Setup

  • Set a single handle on a cable at shoulder height.
  • Stand side-on to the machine, feet wider than shoulder-width.
  • Hold the handle with both hands, fingers laced and arms straight.
  • Brace your trunk before each rep.

How to do it

  1. Push off the foot nearer the machine to shift your weight to your other leg.
  2. At the same time, sweep the handle from above your near shoulder down toward your far hip.
  3. Return the same way, under control, pushing off your far foot.
  4. Do all your reps on one side, then switch.

Cues

  • Arms straight.
  • Chest and belly turn together; back straight.

Common mistakes

  • Bending your arms to pull the handle.
  • Twisting with your shoulders only, instead of turning your chest and belly together.
  • Rounding your back as you turn.

Stop if you feel sharp pain. For an injury or a condition, check with a doctor or physiotherapist.

Dead bug

Also called: Deadbug, Supine dead bug Equipment: Bodyweight Muscles: Abs

Lowering one arm and the opposite leg while lying on your back. It trains your abs to keep your lower back still while your limbs move.

Setup

  • Lie on your back with your knees bent and your feet flat.
  • Pull your shoulders down and back, without arching your lower back or lifting your hips.
  • Brace your trunk lightly, and keep breathing.
  • Lift your arms and legs off the floor: knees over your hips and bent to 90 degrees, elbows over your shoulders.

How to do it

  1. Lower one heel and the opposite hand toward the floor. Touch it lightly, without resting.
  2. Return to the start, then switch to the other arm and leg.

Cues

  • Lower back still.
  • Light touches only.

Common mistakes

  • Arching your lower back as your arm and leg go down.
  • Lifting your hips off the floor.

Stop if you feel sharp pain. For an injury or a condition, check with a doctor or physiotherapist.

Farmer's walk

Also called: Farmer carry, Farmer's carry, Farmers walk, Farmer walk Equipment: Dumbbell, Kettlebell Muscles: Grip, Abs

Walking while holding a heavy weight in each hand. It builds your grip and core strength.

Setup

  • Stand between two dumbbells or kettlebells.
  • Brace your trunk.
  • Grip the handles firmly and lift the weights by driving through your heels, keeping your back straight.
  • Stand tall with your arms straight at your sides.

How to do it

  1. Walk a set distance, keeping your back upright and your head up.
  2. Walk slowly and under control.
  3. Turn around and walk back, then set the weights down the same way you picked them up.

Cues

  • Back upright, head up.
  • Slow, steady steps.

Common mistakes

  • Rounding your back when you pick up or set down the weights.
  • Leaning forward instead of standing tall.
  • Rushing the walk, so you lose control of the weight.

Stop if you feel sharp pain. For an injury or a condition, check with a doctor or physiotherapist.

Floor crunch

Also called: Bodyweight crunch, Basic crunch Equipment: Bodyweight Muscles: Abs

Curling your upper back off the floor. It works your abs.

Setup

  • Lie on your back with your knees bent and your feet flat on the floor.
  • Put your hands behind your head. Squeeze your shoulder blades together and pull your elbows back, without arching your lower back.
  • Brace your trunk before each rep.

How to do it

  1. Curl your ribs toward your hips until your upper back is off the floor. Let your chin tuck slightly.
  2. Keep your feet, tailbone and lower back on the floor.
  3. Lower slowly back down.

Cues

  • Ribs toward hips.
  • Keep your elbows back the whole set.

Common mistakes

  • Not keeping your elbows back the whole set.
  • Arching your lower back.

Stop if you feel sharp pain. For an injury or a condition, check with a doctor or physiotherapist.

Pallof press

Also called: Pallof, Anti-rotation press, Cable anti-rotation press, Standing anti-rotation press, Cable Pallof press Equipment: Cable Muscles: Obliques, Abs

Pressing a cable handle straight out from your chest while the cable pulls you to the side. It trains your trunk to resist twisting.

Setup

  • Set a single handle on a cable at about chest height.
  • Stand side-on to the machine, feet hip-width apart and knees slightly bent.
  • Hold the handle with both hands at your chest, fingers laced and elbows tucked in by your sides.
  • Brace your trunk and stand tall.

How to do it

  1. Press the handle straight out in front of your chest. The cable should run level with your chest.
  2. Hold briefly with your arms straight.
  3. Slowly bring your elbows back to your sides.
  4. Do all your reps on one side, then turn around.

Cues

  • Chest and hips face forward.
  • Back straight and tall.

Common mistakes

  • Letting the cable turn your torso toward the machine.
  • Bringing your elbows back fast instead of slowly.

Stop if you feel sharp pain. For an injury or a condition, check with a doctor or physiotherapist.
