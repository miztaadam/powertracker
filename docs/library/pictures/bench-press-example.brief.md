# Bench press example picture: how it was made

Made 2026-11-12 in the Gemini app (Nano Banana Pro) by the owner, as a trial for the exercise pictures project. Not used in the app yet.

## What worked
1. Start a fresh chat with this prompt (three-quarter view):

> A detailed, anatomically accurate illustration for a strength-training app: two side-by-side panels of the same athlete on the same plain dark charcoal background, 16:9.
>
> Camera: three-quarter view from above and to the side of the bench, near the lifter's feet, so we see the full length of the barbell across his body with a large plate on each end, well out beyond his shoulders on both sides.
>
> The athlete: a fit adult man in a plain grey t-shirt, black shorts and flat training shoes, lying on his back on a flat padded bench inside a power rack. His head, upper back and buttocks stay on the bench, and both feet are planted flat on the floor. Hands are a little wider than shoulder width.
>
> Left panel: the barbell rests across his lower chest at nipple height, touching his shirt. His forearms are vertical under the bar.
>
> Right panel: lockout. His arms are fully straight and the bar is directly over his shoulders.
>
> The rack's safety arms run along both sides of the bench, below the height of his chest.
>
> Style: clean semi-realistic illustration with confident ink outlines and soft cel shading, muted natural colours, and a subtle warm off-white rim light, like a high-quality anatomy textbook figure. Sharp focus throughout. No text, no labels, no watermark, no logos.

2. Then send one targeted edit:

> Keep everything exactly the same in both panels: the athlete, clothing, bench, rack, safety arms, barbell, plates, camera angle, colours, style, lighting and background. Change only the left panel: lower the barbell all the way down until it is resting on his lower chest at nipple height, touching his shirt. His elbows bend deeply and drop slightly below the level of the bench, about 45 degrees out from his body, with his forearms vertical under the bar. The right panel stays unchanged.

## What didn't work
- **Strict side view** (three attempts, including a to-scale pose reference). The model always rested the plate on top of the chest, leaving the bar about 20 cm above it, and set the safety arms above the chest.
- The first three-quarter attempt drew the bottom of the rep half-way down. One edit fixed it.

## Lessons for the full set
- Choose the camera angle per exercise. Bar exercises lying on a bench need a view where the plates sit clear of the body.
- Check every image against its guide: bar position, joints, safety equipment, contact points. Expect one or two edits each.
- Remaining nit here: at the pause the bar sits slightly high on the chest.
